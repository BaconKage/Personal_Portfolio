        02
        
Case study — MyGym

        

          One system for running a gym, replacing the nine apps most of them juggle.
          No jargon below — scroll and the system assembles itself.
        

        
      

    


    

      

        

          
          system architecture
          the problem
        

      


      

        

          01 — the problem
          
Nine apps, none of them talking

          

            A gym runs on WhatsApp for messages, a spreadsheet for leads, Google Forms for
            assessments, a calendar for appointments, separate software for workout plans,
            and accounting software for payments.
          

          

            Each one does its job. The person moving through them is what gets lost.
            A lead never gets called back. What the salesperson promised never reaches the
            coach. Nobody can say what's actually stuck.
          

        


        

          02 — the idea
          
One person, one record

          

            MyGym follows a single member the whole way: first enquiry, consultation, sale,
            payment, onboarding, coaching, progress.
          

          

            Each stage is linked to the one before it. When someone buys a package, their
            coach receives the actual sale — not a copy of it retyped into
            another app. Nothing is entered twice, so nothing drifts out of sync.
          

        


        

          03 — the people
          
Everyone sees only their own job

          

            Sales staff get leads and consultations. Finance sees payments and revenue.
            Coaches get their client queue, plans and check-ins. Members see their own plan
            and nothing else. Managers see what's stuck.
          

          

            One shared set of facts, five different views onto it. A coach never sees
            revenue; a member never sees another member.
          

          

            Web consoleMobile app
            Role-based access
          

        


        

          04 — the build
          
One place that decides what's true

          

            A single service holds every rule — who may see what, which steps are legal, what
            the totals are. The web app and the mobile app both ask it, and neither is
            allowed to invent an answer of its own.
          

          

            Underneath sits a database of 23 linked record types, so any
            check-in can be traced back to the plan, the coach, the sale and the original
            enquiry it came from.
          

          

            TypeScriptNestJSPostgreSQL
            PrismaNext.jsExpo
            Monorepo
          

        


        

          05 — the hard part
          
Refusing to show a wrong number

          

            The genuinely difficult part wasn't features. It was trust. If the server sends
            back something malformed, the app treats it as a failure instead of displaying
            it — because a wrong payment figure does more damage than a visible
            error.
          

          

            It also solves a subtler bug: switch between two members quickly, and a slow
            reply about the first must never land on the second one's screen. Requests are
            tracked and cancelled so information cannot leak across people.
          

          

            Runtime validationRequest cancellation
            Automated tests
          

        


        

          06 — where it stands
          
Demonstrated, heading to pilot

          

            Web app, mobile app, API, database, seeded demo data and automated tests, all
            working end to end. It has completed a full demonstration with a prospective
            first client and is now being hardened into a controlled pilot.
          

          

            Built with a team of three. I'm deliberate about that wording — a demo that
            works and a system safe to run someone's business on are not the same thing, and
            saying so is part of the job.

Research

        
      


      

        PEER-REVIEWED
        AI & Society
        Springer Nature
        Dec 2025
      


      

        As AI systems grow more complex, errors don't disappear —
        they transform into deeper structural uncertainties.
      


      

        
Imperfection as a Constitutive Property of Artificial Intelligence

        

          The paper introduces the Paradox of Imperfection: the observation that
          scaling a system's capability doesn't remove its errors, it relocates them somewhere
          harder to see. It connects AI ethics to complexity theory, and works through the
          consequences in healthcare, legal systems and autonomous vehicles.
        

        

          It closes with governance principles for systems that have to stay trustworthy while
          remaining, by construction, imperfect.
        

        

          AI ethicsComplexity theory
          Explainable AIAI governance
